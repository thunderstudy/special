Section 1

Introduction to Multimedia

Multimedia is a medium of communication that provides information to users in a simple and powerful way. We are all well acquainted with smartphones, computers, and other digital devices. We use these devices for a wide variety of tasks — listening to music, watching videos or movies, playing games, reading animated books, booking tickets, or shopping online.

When we use these devices, everything that appears on screen — pictures, sound, animation, and video — working together simultaneously — that is Multimedia. In simple terms:

The integration (combining) of Text, Graphics, Sound, Video, and Animation is called Multimedia.

The use of multimedia has made computers, smartphones, and other digital devices easier to use. For example, the different app icons on your mobile phone are created using graphics — you tap or click on them and use them. All of this is made possible through multimedia.

In today's world, multimedia is being used extensively across education, entertainment, business, advertising, and many other fields.

Section 2

Definition & Concept of Multimedia

The word Multimedia is made up of two words:

  • Multi — meaning many / multiple
  • Media — a medium of communication through which you can convey your message to others easily

Multimedia is a part of media in which several elements are used together, such as text, image, audio, video, animation, etc. Multimedia presents information in a very easy and attractive way.

In simple words, Multimedia is a technology in which text, data, images, graphics, audio, and video are combined and presented digitally in such a way that the user receives information in a better and more effective manner. Information displayed in the form of pictures and sound is Multimedia.
If you are watching a video on YouTube and someone is providing information through it — you can see a video, images are visible, you can hear the voice/sound, there is audio as well as text — that is the best example of Multimedia.
Section 3

Need / Importance of Multimedia

Multimedia plays an extremely important role in our daily lives. Various forms of multimedia are an inseparable part of our daily routine and are very useful for many other important tasks.

3.1 Entertainment on the Go

If you are listening to songs while travelling, playing mobile games, or watching a cartoon show — all of these are examples of multimedia. Music is a form of multimedia used broadly everywhere, every minute of the day. Even listening to music on the radio can be considered multimedia.

3.2 Communication with People

The main purpose of using multimedia is to communicate easily with people. For example, a radio channel is able to share music with all the people of a state or country. Radio stations also need to play soothing music that is popular with a large number of listeners so that the number of advertisements can increase — which is how they earn money.

3.3 In Education

If you are a teacher, you can use various forms of multimedia to reach your students. Teachers use different types of multimedia tools to explain difficult subjects to students. A teacher can use audio and video lectures, presentations, and other multimedia tools. Students also want variety in teaching methods; they get bored easily if a teacher always discusses a lesson in the same way without any variety. So if you want to make lessons easy and interesting for students, you should use multimedia.

3.4 Business Websites

If you are building a company's website, it is very important that the information on the website is simple, intuitive, and interesting. Websites are powerful tools for attracting customers to a specific product. If you use various forms of multimedia on your website, your visitor will stay longer and browse your entire site. You will have a better chance of them buying or using one of your products. You can choose from good graphics, music files, video files, and even simple mini-games to add to your website.

3.5 Reaching Target Audiences

If communication is one of your work goals, you should definitely use some digital media to help you reach your target audience better. You need to understand that people do not like reading long articles and very detailed product information, so it is better to keep it in audio or video form to attract their attention.

Elements of Multimedia

Multimedia is a collection of many elements such as text, audio, graphics, video, and animation. All multimedia elements are delivered to users in a better and more effective manner through computers. The detailed description of multimedia elements is as follows:

5.1

📝 Text

Used mostly for headings, headlines, menus, etc. Text presents information through letters, numbers, and special characters. Common software: MS Word, Notepad, WordPad. Formats: .doc, .txt, etc.

5.2

🔊 Audio

Sound waves that we can hear. Audio is an analog signal converted to digital via ADC. Includes speech, music, etc. Formats: MPEG, MP3, WAV, MIDI. Software: Sound Forge, Audacity, Audio Cutter.

5.3

🖼️ Graphics

Every multimedia presentation is graphics-based. Graphics make any subject more effectively presentable visually. Software: Adobe Photoshop, CorelDRAW, InDesign. Viewers: Windows Picture Manager, Picasa.

5.4

🎬 Video

Moving pictures with sound — the best way to communicate with each other. Presents information very effectively and saves large amounts of time. Editing software: Adobe Premiere, VideoPad. Formats: MP4, AVI, etc.

5.5

✨ Animation

A series of still graphic images displayed one after another at a certain interval to give the impression of motion. Requires at least 25–30 sequential images per second. Used in advertisements, cartoons, films, and video games. Software: 3D Studio, Adobe Photoshop, Animator Studio.

Audio Players: QuickTime, Real Player, Windows Media Player
Animation Viewers: Internet Explorer, Windows Pictures, Fax Viewer
Section 6

Hardware & Software Requirements for Multimedia

An essential feature of a multimedia computer system is that it should be capable of capturing, editing, and using different types of media (video, audio, etc.), storing all of them, and transmitting all types of data from one computer to another via modem or network.

Since many types of media are involved simultaneously in multimedia, special hardware and software are required to use and create multimedia projects. For using multimedia, a mobile device, laptop, or desktop computer can be used — but designers typically use high-configuration desktop computers for multimedia-related projects, along with other peripheral devices.

6.1 Multimedia Hardware

For multimedia applications, the computer's memory (storage capacity) and processing capacity requirements are greater than those of ordinary computers that can only handle text. Based on their capabilities, multimedia computer systems can be divided into two categories:

i. Multimedia Presentation System ii. Multimedia Development System

All current computer systems and other digital devices such as mobiles and tablets permit multimedia presentation, so they all have the necessary hardware. A computer processor with high processing capacity, high-resolution display screen, speakers, microphone, CD-ROM drive, etc. are essential components of a Multimedia Presentation System.

In a Multimedia Development System, other hardware devices are also used, divided into the following categories:

📼 Analog Media Devices

Input (microphone, video camera, photographic camera), Processing (video/audio effect devices, audio mixer), Storage & Output (speakers, video display, video/audio tape recorders).

💾 Digital Media Devices

Capturing (keyboard, image scanner, OCR device), Processing (video/audio encoders/decoders, digital effect devices), Presentation (printer, monitor, projector).

🗄️ General-Purpose Devices

Storage devices (CD-ROM, floppy drive, hard drive, network interface, general-purpose processors). These are typically all digital.

🖱️ Interaction Devices

Devices that allow users to directly control multimedia applications. Examples: keyboard, mouse, joystick, electronic pen.

6.2 Detailed Hardware Devices

6.2.1 Central Processing Unit (CPU)

The CPU is the most important part of a computer — it is where all processing and synchronisation of tasks occurs. The computer's capability is measured by the speed of data processing. For multimedia use, Pentium Processor IV or higher is preferred.

6.2.2 Graphic Card

A graphic card is connected to the computer's motherboard. When you view pictures or videos or play games on a computer, the graphic card makes them look sharp and clear. It is that part of computer hardware that displays all graphics on the monitor. For each visible element — pictures, video, animation — the graphic card renders it to the display by converting graphical data into signals that the monitor can understand. It is also known as Video Card, Graphic Adapter, or Display Adapter.

For simple multimedia use (watching pictures, videos, or films), you don't need a separate graphics card because it is already built-in to your computer. This built-in graphics card uses the CPU and RAM for multimedia-related processing. However, if you want to run high-graphics software like gaming or video editing, you need to install an additional capacity graphics card. This graphics card has its own RAM and processor, which we call the GPU (Graphical Processing Unit).

6.2.3 Memory & Storage Devices

You need memory to store productions, original audio and video clips, edited audio and video segments, and various files used during multimedia production. You also need memory for backups of your project files.

Primary Memory

Contains only the data and instructions the computer is currently working on. Limited and fast storage. Loses data when computer is switched off. Two types: RAM and ROM.

Cache Memory

Very high-speed semiconductor memory that helps the CPU process at high speed. Acts as a buffer between the CPU and main memory. Stores data and program parts most frequently used by the CPU.

Secondary Memory

Also known as external memory or non-volatile memory (permanently stores data). Slower than main memory. Used for permanent data storage. Examples: Disk, CD-ROM, DVD. Multimedia files generally require more storage space.

6.2.4 Input Devices

⌨️ Keyboard

The most common input device. Helps input data into the computer. Layout is similar to a traditional typewriter. Available in 84, 101/102, and now 104/108-key versions. Special multimedia keyboards also available for volume control, gaming, etc.

🖱️ Mouse

An optical computer mouse uses a light source (LED) and a light detector to track movement relative to the surface. Typically has two buttons (left and right) and a scroll bar in the middle. Used to control the cursor on screen; cannot be used to enter text.

🕹️ Joystick

A pointing device used to move the cursor's position on the monitor screen. It is a rod with round balls at both the lower and upper ends. The lower ball moves in a socket. Can be moved in all four directions. Mainly used in Computer-Aided Design (CAD) and computer games.

✒️ Light Pen

A pointing device similar to a pen. Used to select displayed menu items or draw pictures on the monitor screen. It has a photocell and an optical system. When the tip is moved to the monitor screen and the button is pressed, the photocell sensing element detects the screen location and sends the relevant signal to the CPU. Mainly used with design tablets.

📐 Digitizer

A hardware device that receives analog information — such as sound or light — and records it digitally. The information is stored in a file on a computing device. This process is called digitization. Example: A digital camera is a digitizer — light enters through the lens and hardware/software converts it to binary data stored as an image file.

🖨️ Scanner

An input device that works like a photocopier machine. Used when information available on paper needs to be transferred to the computer's hard disk for modification. The scanner captures the image from the source, which is converted to digital form and stored on disk. These images can be edited before printing.

🎙️ Microphone

An input device for sound input which is stored in digital form. Used as a standalone mic or integrated with headphones. Used for adding sound to multimedia presentations or mixing music.

🔊 Speaker

An output device that produces sound stored in digital form. Used for adding sound to multimedia presentations and displaying films. All modern computer monitors have integrated speakers; mobile devices also have integrated speakers.

📷 Digital Camera

An input device for capturing images stored in digital form. Used for adding images to multimedia presentations or for personal purposes.

🎥 Digital Video Camera

An input device for capturing pictures/videos stored in digital form. Used for adding video to multimedia presentations or for personal purposes.

6.2.5 Output Devices

🖥️ Flat-Panel Display Monitor

Refers to a class of video devices with less volume, weight, and power requirements than CRT. Can be hung on walls or worn on wrists. Used in calculators, video games, monitors, laptops, graphics displays. Divided into: Emissive (plasma panels, LED) and Non-emissive (LCD).

🖨️ Printer

An electronic device used to print digital information on paper. Three types: Dot Matrix Printer (prints by joining dots like characters, has 24 pins), Laser Printer (produces high-quality images for office/business use using a laser beam and toner), Image Projector (projects information from a computer onto a large screen for a group to view simultaneously).

🔉 Sound Card & Speakers

The computer needs both a sound card and speakers to listen to audio like music, speech, and sound effects. Most motherboards provide an on-board sound card. The main function of the sound card is to convert digital sound signals to analog for the speaker, which we can increase or decrease as needed.

🥽 VR Headset

For gaming technology, Virtual Reality headsets and other devices are also included in multimedia application hardware.

6.3 Multimedia Software

Software is a collection of instructions or programs that direct the computer to perform a specific task. Multimedia software works with hardware to use and produce media elements. Various software is available to use and produce these media elements (films, sound, text, animation, graphics, etc.) such as: Paint Brush, Photo Finish, Animator, Photoshop, 3D Studio, Corel Draw, Sound Forge, Adobe Premiere, Apple HyperCard, Photo Magic, Picture Publisher, etc.

Section 7

Multimedia Applications & Tools

Text, graphics, animation, audio, and video are integral parts of multimedia. Various software tools are available for producing and editing these media elements. The categories of basic software tools are as follows:

7.1 Text Editing Tools

These tools are used to create letters, resumes, invoices, purchase orders, user manuals for projects, and other documents such as newspapers and printed books. Word processing software like MS Word is an excellent example of a text tool. All word processing software has additional features along with the main word processing functions.

Features of MS Word include:
• Create new files, open saved files, save files, and print
• Insert symbols, formulas, and equations into files
• Correct spelling and grammar mistakes
• Align text within margins
• Insert page numbers at top or bottom of page
• Mail merge documents and create letters, envelopes
• Create tables by entering number of columns and rows

7.2 Painting & Drawing Tools

These tools usually come with a graphical user interface with pull-down menus for quick selection. You can create almost all types of possible shapes and resize them using these tools. Drawing files can be imported or exported in many image formats like .gif, .tif, .jpg, .bmp, etc.

🎨 Examples: CorelDraw, Freehand, Designer, Photoshop, Fireworks
Features include: Tools for drawing straight lines, rectangular areas, circles, etc.  |  Option to select various colors  |  Pencil tool for drawing freehand shapes  |  Eraser tool for removing parts of an image  |  Zooming for magnified pixel editing

7.3 Image Editing Tools

Editing tools are used to edit or reshape existing images and pictures. These tools can be used to create images from scratch as well as from original artwork files created with scanners, digital cameras, clip art files, or painting and drawing tools.

📸 Examples: Adobe Photoshop, Paint Shop Pro

7.4 Sound Editing Tools

These tools are used to very easily integrate sound into multimedia projects. You can copy, paste, and edit segments of a sound file using these tools. The presence of sound mostly enhances the effect of a graphic presentation — especially in video.

🎵 Examples: Cool Edit Pro, Sound Forge, Pro Tools
Features include:
• Record your own music, voice, or any other audio
• Record sound from CD, DVD, radio, or any other sound player
• Mix and edit sound with any other audio
• Apply special effects like Equalizer, Echo, Reverse

7.5 Video Editing Tools

These tools are used to edit, cut, copy, and paste video and audio files. Video editing requires expensive special equipment, understanding, and training. Video editing is an artistic process in which you decide which elements received from various sources should be kept, removed, or added so that the edited video looks integrated, logical, and planned.

Today computers are powerful enough to handle this work, disk space is cheap, and it is very easy to store and distribute completed work on DVD.

🎬 Examples: Adobe Premiere, Adobe After Effects — and many other software with their own specific capabilities and limitations

7.6 Animation & Modelling Tools

Still images are displayed at a fixed rate using animation and modelling tools to give visual effects — this technique is called animation. These tools have features like multiple windows that allow you to see your model in each dimension, the ability to drag and drop basic shapes into the scene, colour and texture mapping, and the ability to add realistic effects like transparency, shading, and fog to scenes.

🎭 Examples: Blender, 3D Animation & Modelling Tool, 3D Studio Max, Maya — and many others with their own specific capabilities and limitations
Section 8

Creating Simple Multimedia with PowerPoint

PowerPoint is an application software used especially for presentations. For effective presentations, PowerPoint has the capability to integrate all multimedia elements into a presentation. Tools are available in PowerPoint to add multimedia elements. You can crop images, trim video clips, and adjust audio levels.

💡 PowerPoint's built-in multimedia tools are sufficient for creating presentations — you generally don't need additional multimedia applications.

8.1 Images in PowerPoint: Still & Animated

The simplest way to add an image to PowerPoint is to just copy and paste it. You can copy any image to your clipboard and then right-click to paste it into PowerPoint.

1
Via Insert MenuGo to Insert menu → click on Picture menu → browse and select the image → insert
2
Multiple Images at OnceHold Control key on keyboard and click on several images → they will all insert into one slide simultaneously
3
Resize & MoveOnce an image is inserted, click on it to drag it around the slide. Click and drag the corner to resize the image as needed.

8.2 PowerPoint Picture Tools

PowerPoint has a set of tools to edit images within the application. You don't always need to use Photoshop or another image editor to make basic changes to your images. When you select an image, a new option appears in the PowerPoint ribbon — click on Picture Tools to see the image editing tools format.

8.3 Audio in PowerPoint

Audio is another type of multimedia that you can work with in PowerPoint. If you have an MP3 or WAV audio file on your computer, you can easily add it to your presentation.

1
Insert AudioClick Insert → Audio → select the audio file saved on your computer
2
Play During PresentationAfter selecting an audio file, a small speaker icon appears on your slide. Hover over it and press the play button to play it while presenting.

8.4 Audio Recording in PowerPoint

If you don't have a pre-recorded audio file, you can record it within PowerPoint itself.

1
Start RecordingGo to Insert menu → Audio → click Record Audio option
2
RecordPress the red Record button and PowerPoint will start recording audio
3
Stop & SaveWhen you've finished recording, press the Stop button (black square) to add the audio to PowerPoint

8.5 Video in PowerPoint

Video is a powerful type of multimedia that can make your PowerPoint presentation more impactful. PowerPoint allows you to insert video from your computer.

PowerPoint supports most major video file types including MP4, QuickTime, and Windows Media files. The new version of PowerPoint also allows inserting online videos from YouTube.

Once the video is on your slide, you can drag the corners to resize it and control how much of the slide it covers.

8.6 Animation in PowerPoint

Animation can help make a PowerPoint presentation more dynamic and information more memorable. Among all types of animation effects, Entrance and Exit are the most common. You can also add sound to intensify your animation effects.

⚠️ Expert Tip: Presentation experts advise using animation and sound effects sparingly. Too much animation can distract — it should be used just enough to make the presentation dynamic and support your subject, not so much that it diverts the audience's attention from what you are saying.

8.7 Slide Animation Effects

You can animate objects on your PowerPoint slides. PowerPoint offers four types of animation:

Type 1

🚪 Entrance

Determines how an object is displayed on a slide — for example, an object can move onto the slide.

Type 2

⭐ Emphasis

Does something to draw attention to an object — for example, the object grows larger.

Type 3

🚶 Exit

Determines how an object leaves the slide — for example, an object can fly off the slide.

Type 4

🔄 Motion Paths

Determines how an object moves around a slide — for example, an object can move from left to right.

Section 9

Applications of Multimedia Across Various Industries

A multimedia application is one that uses text, graphics, pictures, sound/audio, animation, and video from multiple media sources. A multimedia application is an exciting and innovative multimedia system that communicates information to users according to their needs. In today's world of Information Technology, multimedia is being used extensively in every field.

9.1 Entertainment

The entertainment industry has used multimedia technology to create games that look like real life. Many developers have used graphics, sound, and animation of multimedia to create various types of games. Special technologies like Virtual Reality have made these games just like real-life experiences. For example, flight simulator games are built with virtual reality technology that provides a real-life imaging experience.

Many multimedia games are now available on computers. Children can enjoy these experiences — for example, they can drive various types of cars, fly aircraft, play any musical instrument, play golf, etc.

Television show set designing, integration and presentation of many types of multimedia elements in films — all of these have made extensive use of multimedia in entertainment. Old black-and-white films and pictures are being colourised using multimedia. Old recorded audio is being converted digitally and stored. Depictions like dinosaurs of enormous size or armies of millions of men, cars and people flying in the air, or showing characters in icy valleys or flying through the air while sitting in a studio — all of these are being made possible through multimedia. Platforms like Netflix and Amazon Prime present digital content created using multimedia.

9.2 Education

Multimedia is used in education for computer-based training courses (commonly called CBT) and for producing reference books like encyclopaedias. A CBT user receives various types of information in a simple way through different types of presentations, tests on specific subjects, and images.

Edutainment is an informal term used for the combination of education with entertainment, especially multimedia entertainment. Multimedia applications are being used as alternatives to printed textbooks. The utility and demand for educational multimedia applications has both increased greatly. Multimedia applications have greatly influenced the field of education — creating study material with greater clarity and appropriate examples and displaying them makes things more interesting and easy to understand through audio-visual media. Practical activities can be easily explained and understood by displaying them through animation.

9.3 Journalism

In journalism, a journalist uses text, images, sound, video, and graphics to tell an interesting story. Journalists often work for newspapers, radio, or television stations, or use computers in social media. They sample animation, data, video, graphics, and audio. Multimedia is used on a large scale in journalism. Journalists read documents, observe events, and conduct interviews with people. They take notes, tape interviews, and verify information about the stories they are working on. They often use video cameras, sound systems, and editing equipment to broadcast their thoughts on specific stories. They sometimes make public comments through social media like Twitter or Facebook. Sometimes journalists resort to animation to verify their stories where no type of image or video can be obtained.

9.4 Multimedia on the Web

In today's world of Information Technology, we are experiencing various multimedia through the web. It is a broadcasting medium offering various online facilities such as live TV, pre-recorded video, photos, animation, etc. In the coming years, most multimedia application experiences will be on the Internet.

Plug-ins and media player software programs provide us with multimedia experience on the web. The file formats requiring this software are known as MIME (Multimedia Internet Mail Extension).
